5 Signs Your Child Needs Extra Academic Support

Most parents notice something is off long before the school does. These five patterns show up consistently in children who are quietly falling behind, regardless of how hard they are working.

"Just five more minutes" stretches into two hours

Homework that should take 20 minutes regularly becomes a two-hour ordeal. This is almost never about laziness or distraction. It usually means something foundational is missing, and every new concept is being built on unstable ground.

"She knows the answer, she just can't hold it all in her head at once."

They've quietly stopped putting their hand up

A child who was once curious and eager begins to disengage. They stop volunteering answers, avoid eye contact with teachers, and report school as "fine" without detail. This is self-protection. When effort consistently fails to produce results, children stop exposing themselves to failure.

"He used to love maths. I don't know what happened."

Their marks and their potential are telling two different stories

You watch your child at home, quick, curious, capable, and you see the gap between who they are and what their grades show. That distance is almost never about intelligence. It is almost always about specific concepts that were never properly closed.

"I know she's smart. The teachers just don't see it."

More effort, no improvement

When a child is revising harder than ever but their results are not moving, the strategy is not working. Effort applied to the wrong thing is wasted effort. There is usually a specific earlier concept, one they nodded through, that is now blocking everything built on top of it.

"He revised all weekend. He still failed."

The curriculum gap is quietly widening

Children in diaspora families often move between school systems mid-journey. What is covered at age 9 in Nigeria may not appear in the UK, Canada, or Australia until age 11. That two-year gap in fractions, algebra, and comprehension techniques compounds silently. A child who was thriving at home suddenly struggles abroad, through no fault of their own.

"She was top of her class in Lagos. Two years later, she's struggling."
