Question 1 · Exchange Rate
The Currency Calculation
If 1 British Pound equals approximately 2,100 Naira, and a family budgets £145 for a holiday project, how many Naira is that in total?
£145 × 2,100 = 304,500 Naira
Tests multi-digit multiplication fluency and real-world currency application.
Question 2 · Order of Operations
The Bakery Order
Aunty Titi orders 5 boxes of meat pies. Each box has 6 pies. She already had 8 extra pies at home and gives away 14 pies to neighbours.

Write the expression and solve it. How many pies does she end up with?
Expression: (5 × 6) + 8 − 14 = 30 + 8 − 14 = 24 pies
Tests strict adherence to BODMAS: multiplication first, then left-to-right addition/subtraction.
Part 2: Fractions, Decimals & Proportions

Work through each step in order and show your reasoning.

Problem A · Step 1 of 3
The Ankara Discount Dilemma
A merchant in a London boutique is having a clearance sale. An Ankara fabric piece originally costs £80.

Step 1: The merchant offers a 1/4 discount off the original price. What is the discount amount in pounds?
1/4 of £80 = 80 ÷ 4 = £20 discount
Problem A · Step 2 of 3
The Ankara Discount Dilemma (continued)
Step 2: What is the final sale price of one fabric piece after the discount is applied?
£80 − £20 = £60 per piece
Problem A · Step 3 of 3
The Ankara Discount Dilemma (continued)
Step 3: A customer buys two of these discounted fabric pieces and pays with a £150 note. How much change do they receive?
2 × £60 = £120  →  £150 − £120 = £30 change

Problem B · Task 1 of 2
The Flight Luggage Weight Puzzle
Kemi packs two identical suitcases for a flight to Lagos. Both suitcases together, plus a 5 kg carry-on backpack, weigh a total of 45 kg.

Let x = the weight of one suitcase.
Task 1: Write an equation representing this total weight.
Equation: 2x + 5 = 45
Both suitcases (2x) plus the backpack (5) equals total weight (45).
Problem B · Task 2 of 2
The Flight Luggage Weight Puzzle (continued)
Using your equation from Task 1:  2x + 5 = 45

Task 2: Solve for x to find the weight of a single suitcase. Is she within the 30 kg per-suitcase limit?
Subtract 5 from both sides: 2x = 40
Divide by 2: x = 20 kg per suitcase
Yes — 20 kg is within the 30 kg airline limit.

Question 8 · Fractions & Percentages
The Summer Camp Survey
50 diaspora children at a cultural summer camp were asked about their favourite meal:
  • Jollof Rice: 25 students
  • Pounded Yam & Egusi: 15 students
  • Suya & Plantain: 10 students

What fraction of students chose Jollof Rice? Express it as a percentage.
Fraction: 25/50 = 1/2  →  Percentage: 50%
Tests fraction simplification and fraction-to-percentage conversion.
