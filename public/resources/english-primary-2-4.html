Part 1: Vocabulary in Context

Read each sentence carefully. Choose the correct word or identify the right answer.

Question 1 · Homophones
The Market Vocabulary
“Aunty Bisi walked through the noisy, vibrant market, carrying a heavy basket of ___________ mangoes.”
  • (a) ripe
  • (b) write
  • (c) right
(a) ripe — Tests homophones and contextual spelling. “Ripe” describes fruit; “write” and “right” are unrelated in this context.
Question 2 · Past Tense
Irregular Verbs
“Yesterday, we ___________ (go / went) to the community centre to celebrate Independence Day.”

Which is correct: go or went?
went — Tests irregular past-tense verbs. “Yesterday” signals the past, so we need the irregular form went, not go.
Question 3 · Prefixes
Prefixes & Opposites
What is the correct opposite of the word “obedient” when adding a prefix (un-, dis-, mis-)?
Disobedient — “dis-” is the correct prefix. Tests morphological awareness and vocabulary expansion. (Note: “unobedient” is not standard English.)
Part 2: Reading Comprehension

Read the passage carefully, then answer the questions below in complete sentences.

Kofi’s First Snow
Eight-year-old Kofi stared out the frosted window of his new apartment in Toronto. Just three months ago, he was running barefoot under the bright, blazing sun in Accra, chasing mango skins with his cousins. Today, the world outside was completely white, silent, and freezing.

“Put on your heavy boots, your woolen scarf, and your thick jacket, Kofi!” called Mama from the kitchen, holding a steaming mug of Milo.

Kofi sighed, missing the warmth of his homeland. But as he stepped outside and his boots crunched against the fresh snow for the very first time, a wide smile spread across his face. He caught a delicate snowflake on his glove. Maybe this new winter adventure wasn’t so bad after all.
Question 4 · Literal Retrieval
Where is Kofi’s story set?
What two cities or countries are contrasted in Kofi’s story?
Accra (Ghana) and Toronto (Canada). Kofi moved from the warmth of Accra to the winter of Toronto.
Question 5 · Inference
Reading Between the Lines
Why did Kofi sigh when his mother told him to put on his heavy winter gear?
He was feeling homesick. He missed the warmth and familiarity of Accra. The winter clothing reminded him how different his new life was from home.
Question 6 · Vocabulary in Action
Describing Words
Find one descriptive word (adjective) in the first paragraph that describes the sun in Accra, and one that describes the snow in Toronto.
Sun: blazing (or “bright”)
Snow: white, silent, or freezing
Tests identifying descriptive adjectives and understanding sensory language.
Part 3: Creative Writing Challenge

This section is for your child to write. Use the parent checklist below to evaluate their response.

Writing Prompt
The Grand Celebration
Write a short paragraph of 4–5 sentences describing a family festival. It could be a naming ceremony, Eid, Christmas, or a cultural wedding.

Describe the sounds, the smells of the food, and how you felt. Try to use at least two connecting words like: because, although, suddenly, meanwhile.
Parent Evaluation Checklist
  • Every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with correct punctuation.
  • At least 3 descriptive sensory words are used (e.g. aromatic, rhythmic, colourful).
  • Ideas are connected smoothly using conjunctions or connecting words.
  • The paragraph stays on topic and describes a real event or celebration.
