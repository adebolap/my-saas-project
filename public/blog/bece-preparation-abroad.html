How to Prepare Your Child for BECE and Nigerian Exams from Abroad

11 July 2026 · 7 min read · By ThinkViva

Your daughter is thirteen, living in Toronto, and doing well at her Canadian school. But you have always planned that she will sit the Basic Education Certificate Examination before the family possibly returns to Nigeria, or simply because you want her to hold that credential. You start looking into it and realise, with some anxiety, that the BECE syllabus looks quite different from what her school teaches. You are not the first diaspora parent to feel that concern, and you are definitely not without options.

Preparing for the BECE and other Nigerian exams from abroad is entirely achievable with the right structure and the right support. This article walks you through what the examinations involve, why Nigerian curriculum alignment matters, and how families across the UK, Canada, USA, and Australia are helping their children succeed.

What Is the BECE?

The Basic Education Certificate Examination is the standardised test taken at the end of Junior Secondary School 3 (JS3) in Nigeria, roughly equivalent to Grade 9. It is administered by the National Examinations Council (NECO) and assesses students across a range of subjects, including English Language, Mathematics, Basic Science, Social Studies, Civic Education, Business Studies, and Cultural and Creative Arts. Passing the BECE is the gateway to Senior Secondary School in Nigeria and is an increasingly valued credential for diaspora children who plan to enrol in Nigerian institutions later in life or who may one day relocate.

Why the Curriculum Gap Is Real

A child who has been schooled entirely in the British, Canadian, American, or Australian system will have covered much of the same mathematical and scientific content as their Nigerian peers, but the framing, terminology, and emphasis will differ in important ways. The Nigerian school curriculum for diaspora families can feel unfamiliar not because the content is harder, but because the way questions are structured, the style of worked examples, and the examination technique expected are all shaped by a specific Nigerian tradition.

For example, BECE English papers place a strong emphasis on formal letter writing, essay composition in prescribed formats, and comprehension passages that reflect Nigerian contexts and idioms. A child who has only practised British or Canadian writing styles may struggle with these, not because of any gap in intelligence, but simply because they have never been taught that particular format. The same applies to Mathematics, where the Nigerian curriculum uses specific methods and notation that can feel foreign even to a mathematically confident child.

JS3 Exam Preparation: What Good Looks Like

Effective JS3 exam preparation for children living abroad typically involves three distinct elements:

  • Curriculum mapping: Identifying exactly which topics the child has already covered in their local school and which Nigerian curriculum topics need to be introduced or reinforced from scratch
  • Past paper practice: Working through genuine BECE past questions under timed conditions so that the format becomes familiar and exam anxiety diminishes
  • Examination technique: Understanding how to allocate time across sections, how to structure essays and formal letters, and how to approach both multiple-choice and theory questions

This kind of targeted preparation is best delivered by someone who knows the Nigerian examination system from the inside, a tutor who has themselves taught to BECE standard, worked through past papers, and understands what NECO examiners look for in a high-scoring response.

The Role of a Nigeria-Based Tutor

Online tutoring from Nigeria-based teachers offers a distinct advantage for BECE preparation abroad. These tutors work within the Nigerian curriculum every day. They know the current NECO syllabus, they have access to up-to-date past papers and marking schemes, and they bring the classroom experience that turns confused students into confident ones. Sessions are held via video call, making the physical distance between Lagos and London, or Abuja and Adelaide, largely irrelevant.

Keeping Up with the Nigerian School Curriculum from Abroad

Even if your child is not sitting the BECE imminently, maintaining alignment with the Nigerian school curriculum as a diaspora family keeps important options open. Children who stay connected to the curriculum find it far easier to:

  • Transition smoothly into a Nigerian school if the family relocates, temporarily or permanently
  • Engage meaningfully with cousins and peers during visits to Nigeria
  • Sit later Nigerian examinations such as WAEC or NECO at the Senior Secondary level without a gap year of catch-up
  • Maintain cultural and academic confidence in both their host country and their heritage country

Practical Tips for Parents

Here is what experienced diaspora parents recommend:

  • Start early: Do not wait until JS3. Consistent tutoring from JS1 (Grade 7) onwards makes the BECE far less daunting when it arrives
  • Build a weekly routine: Two sessions per week of 60 to 90 minutes each provides steady progress without overwhelming a child already attending full-time school abroad
  • Mix academic and language subjects: Pairing a core subject (Maths or English) with a heritage language lesson (Yoruba, Igbo, or Hausa) in each tutoring week maintains cultural engagement alongside academic preparation
  • Review past papers together: Sitting with your child for 20 minutes while they work through an old BECE question reinforces good exam habits and keeps you informed of their progress
  • Ask for regular updates: A good tutor will communicate proactively, but do ask for monthly written progress notes if they are not offered automatically
